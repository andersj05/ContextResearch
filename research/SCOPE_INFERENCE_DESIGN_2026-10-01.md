# Testing automatic retention contracts from actual decision code

October 1, 2026. Design and bounded source review; model outcomes pending.
The user authorized continuing research and online Luna calls. The separate
[development protocol](../experiments/dependency_memory/scope_inference/PROTOCOL.md)
proposes eight source-only contract-inference calls, without reopening any old
allocation or relabeling inspected evidence as natural prospective tasks.

## Why this target

The [receipt audit](WORKFLOW_METHOD_AUDIT_2026-10-01.md) showed that checking a
complete known table adds no receipt information over direct projection. It
also showed that exact-snapshot reporting cannot use A evidence from other trees.
The earlier [historical replay](HISTORICAL_WORKFLOW_REPLAY_2026-09-22.md) already
implements authored dependency-scoped reuse. The unresolved part is automatically
obtaining a complete useful contract rather than supplying it by hand.

Use the actual historical dispatch/accounting classifier, not an invented
workflow. Its decisions depend on values, dictionary types, key membership and
whether metadata is empty. A projection can preserve the obvious positive fields
yet turn nonempty unrecognized metadata into an empty dictionary, incorrectly
claiming no dispatch. It can also discard evidence that contradicts a `false`
dispatch flag. These are hypotheses to test against the complete frozen catalog;
no new incident or model failure is claimed yet.

The learner receives only the original public decision code and a constrained
projection language. The handwritten projection, records and evaluator tests
are withheld from its serialized request. The author has inspected those inputs:
this is source-driven development evaluation, not blind human review or unseen
workflow generalization. No model-generated Python is executed.

The learned contracts face handwritten direct retention, conservative AST root
retention, raw history, and cached program outputs. Caching is especially strong
because the public classifier is known before the final record query. Any method
claim must survive that control and account for extraction, not merely show that
an inferred schema is smaller than raw JSON.

## Bounded primary-source comparison

Mokhov, Mitchell and Peyton Jones, *Build Systems à la Carte*, ICFP 2018,
DOI 10.1145/3236774: the [author-hosted final manuscript](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems-final.pdf)
was read in Sections 2.2 and 4.2.2–4.2.4 on October 1. Verifying traces record
dependency hashes; constructive traces also retain results, allowing reuse
when dependencies match. Dynamic dependencies and task changes are part of
that established setting. Full proofs and implementation replication were
not reviewed here. The 2020 journal revision was not read.

Our inference: scope-bound evidence and cached outcomes are established ideas,
so those mechanisms alone cannot supply a novelty claim. The narrow research
question is whether a charged model can infer a complete compact input contract
for a real decision program and how omissions behave under a later query.
This distinction does not establish priority or practical advantage.

The primary [Build Scripts with Perfect Dependencies manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.12737)
was also inspected in Sections 2.3–2.4 and 3.4 for tracing/reuse and platform
limits. Its input/output tracing is another close mechanism precedent. We have
not completed publication-metadata verification or its formal proofs, so it
remains a reading lead rather than an upgraded bibliography entry.

## Planned evidence and limits

Record every model proposal and its exact source/request identity. Preserve
syntax failures, semantic failures, dangerous false-preflight decisions,
oversized states and missing calls. Compare both prompts on the same catalog,
and enumerate all late worker-pair/record queries after two byte boundaries.
Final decoding uses the public program and is scripted; the only online calls
infer the contract. Eight replicates concern one program, not eight tasks.

The criterion is declared in the protocol before generation. A failure, tie or
an advantage erased by the zero-model controls is a valid result. General
semantic compilation, native compaction, independent natural-task prevalence,
server revision and production total-cost improvement remain open.

Before generation, the offline staged controls already show an important limit:
the handwritten JSON projection succeeds on 12/24 queries because three child
serializations exceed 1,536 bytes, while cached public-program results fit all
boundaries and succeed on 24/24. Conservative whole-root and raw-record memory
exceed the parent cap. These are constructed encoding comparisons on inspected
records. They do not establish an information-theoretic obstruction; the existing
fixed-schema codec is another relevant zero-model serialization control.

Prelaunch validation: all 461 offline tests pass, repository validation reports
PASS with 47 bibliography entries and 57 claims, and paper assembly resolves 39
cited sources. Four local mock transport cases pass on CLI 0.159.2, with no real
model generation. The prepared allocation pins 23 source/protocol/dependency
files and eight request identities. Provider outcomes and independent human
review remain pending at this design milestone.
