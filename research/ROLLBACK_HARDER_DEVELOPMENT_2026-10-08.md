# Harder-task rollback development study

October 8, 2026. Closed with gaps after one unknown terminal dispatch. Five
completed math pairs show one benefit, one drawback and three ties for deleting
the failed branch; their mean R-minus-K is zero. Neither superiority nor
equivalence is established.

The [earlier subscription pilot](ROLLBACK_SUBSCRIPTION_2026-10-08.md) produced
only one eligible math recovery pair and no coding pairs. It did not establish
an advantage or equivalence for deleting failed history. The user authorized
continued work to find useful results using GPT-6 Luna through their existing
Codex subscription, with no token/cost ceiling.

The [new frozen protocol](../experiments/dependency_memory/rollback_recovery_v3/PROTOCOL.md)
defines 64 new original constructed tasks: 32 coupled math instances and 32
Python instances from eight algorithmic families with four variants each. Initial
checkpoint/branch effort is low; both writers and all four recovery arms are
medium. Task complexity and initial effort both change, so any change in failure
frequency cannot be attributed to effort alone. K/R still change only failed
branch retention within each task, sharing the checkpoint, public failure and
lesson exactly.

Preparation evidence lives in
[the dated package](../experiments/dependency_memory/results/rollback_recovery_v3_2026-10-08/).
Independent math formulations agree on 1,111,296 finite candidates. All 416
reference executions pass the frozen coding suites in digest-pinned isolated
Docker. Nineteen installed-client probes verify low/medium settings, empty
ephemeral workspaces, the existing global instruction channel, larger request
bodies and the no-retry single-response guard. These are implementation checks,
not observations of model recovery or authenticated provider internals.

The eight controller/analysis invariants pass. The additional six-mutation archive
test initially exposed two Windows fixture-cleanup errors from unclosed SQLite
connections; the original failed check is retained. After closing those fixture
connections, all six probes pass in a new source-bound check. All 709 repository
tests pass without skips. Full scientific validation passes and the paper builds
with its existing 75 claims and 48 cited sources.

The entire allocation is fixed before outcomes: eight excluded calibration calls
and 832 possible study calls across four sequential accounting blocks. A public
pass ends the initial attempt even if reserved grading fails. Malformed records,
no candidate, unusable writers, unknown dispatch and every unperformed slot stay
in the report. Unknown metering stops all later blocks. No task replacements or
forced wrong answers are allowed.

Report primary paired recovery separately for math and coding, plus initial
quality, all arm totals, missingness bounds, exact rejected-candidate repetition
and resources. Coding variants share templates, so descriptive family resampling
requires at least six represented complete eligible families. Two terminal draws
are repeated measurements on a task. Optional independent human ratings remain
unperformed. Any result describes this development cohort; it does not establish
benchmark performance or improvements over native compaction. The original
LiveCodeBench proposal and later policy/confirmation stages remain unperformed.

The complete cohort and all identities were frozen in commit `3c8a1ee` before
live outcomes. All eight separate subscription calibration calls completed with
valid formats and complete usage; they are excluded from study outcomes. The
study stopped after thirteen tasks started. Twelve completed their episodes:
six coding candidates passed public and reserved suites, five math tasks
completed all four recovery arms, and one modular task returned a malformed
candidate record. The sixth eligible math task stopped on its first terminal
request, Z draw 1, with the archived error class `ProtocolError`. The reason code
was not retained and cannot be reconstructed. Fifty-one tasks never started.
All 64 task dispositions and 832 possible study slots remain in the archive;
no call or later block was retried to bypass the stop.

answer exactly repeats a rejected candidate. The first frozen-order benefit,
drawback and tie are shown above; neither direction was the stopping criterion.
The inspected lesson for coloring-16 identifies the violated allowed-color list
without supplying a solution; this is an unblinded case observation, not an
independent human rating.

The six scheduled math pairs have fixed-denominator R-minus-K missingness bounds
[-16.67, +16.67] pp. These bounds do **not** cover the 51 unstarted tasks or their
unknown eligibility. The full-cohort effect is unidentified. The protocol's
six-complete-pair screen fails: only five pairs complete. The raw inherited field
`feasibility_screen_at_least_six_primary_tasks` counts scheduled pairs and is true
for math; it must not be read as passing the complete-pair screen. Coding has no
recovery pair. Bootstrap and family intervals are unavailable; there is no
confirmatory p-value.

The unresolved call retains its 4.225-credit conditional reservation. These are

past protocol failure nor resolves its reservation.

The next transport version should retain whitelisted protocol reason codes
without raw provider/session logs. Any follow-up requires its own frozen source,
cohort and allocation; it cannot resume this closed study or erase its gaps.
